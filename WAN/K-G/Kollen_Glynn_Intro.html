Tax planning · The tax calendar of the transition

The same dollar of income costs very different amounts in different years. The plan puts each kind of income in its cheapest year, and Washington's rules change the order.

2026 to 2027 · 37% years

Defer, deduct, and do not sell

  • 401(k) $24,500 plus the $8,000 Roth catch-up; after-tax Roth if offered; HSA
  • Deferred compensation election, if SAP has a plan
  • Charitable giving bunched into a donor-advised fund or CRT with appreciated shares, if there is intent
  • No option exercises and no voluntary gains: 20% + 3.8% + Washington's 7% to 9.9%
2028 to 2029 · exit years

Fill the low brackets on purpose

  • C.H. Robinson options in three tranches (2028, 2029, January 2030): ordinary income, never subject to Washington's capital gains tax
  • Gain ladder begins: about $278K of gains a year stays under Washington's exemption, taxed near 15%
  • About $218K saved on today's Fidelity gains versus selling while employed
2030 to 2037 · business years

Let the business carry the deductions

  • Asset purchase: equipment expensed under 100% bonus depreciation; goodwill over 15 years
  • A business retirement plan for Kollen and staff: a large deduction
  • Roth conversions from the IRA annuity in the lowest years
  • Social Security deferred to 70: $655K more over a lifetime
2038 onward · Medicare years

Manage the income tests

  • Medicare premiums rise with income two years earlier: plan conversions and sales around those brackets
  • Required distributions start at 75
  • Policy loans stay tax-free only if the policies never lapse: monitor yearly

Three Washington taxes that shape the plan

No income taxSalary, option exercises, interest and Roth conversions face federal tax only.
Capital gains excise7% on long-term gains above about $278K a year, 9.9% above $1M. Retirement accounts and real estate are exempt. Hence the gain ladder.
Estate tax up to 35%Above a $3M exclusion, with no gift tax. Lifetime gifts and an ILIT are worth more here than in almost any other state.
B&O taxA Washington business pays tax on gross receipts, not profit: price it into the deal.

California, if it enters the picture

  • Up to 13.3% on all income, capital gains included
  • No QSBS exclusion and no federal bonus depreciation
  • Taxes non-residents on California-source business income
  • No estate tax
Buying a California business, or moving there, changes the plan. Buying in Washington keeps it intact. Decide residency and the target's location together.

Choose the entity before the letter of intent

LLC or S corporationOne layer of tax, the 20% qualified business income deduction, losses usable. Best when the business funds Kollen's income.
C corporation21% corporate rate; QSBS can exclude up to $15M of federal gain on a sale (50% after 3 years, 100% after 5) for non-service businesses. Best when the plan is to grow and sell.
The CPA models both on the real target's numbers.
What the calendar is worth, on today's numbers: about $218K saved on the Fidelity gains by selling after salary stops, about $100K to $150K on the option exercises, $655K more lifetime Social Security by waiting to 70, and about $2.8M less estate tax from the ILIT. None of it requires taking more risk; all of it requires doing things in order.

Business acquisition · Ownership structure, protection and succession

Hover any box for what it does and why it is there

How to buy: purchase structures

Asset purchasePreferred. New LLC buys the assets; liabilities stay with the seller; stepped-up basis and depreciation.
Stock purchaseOnly if licences or contracts cannot move. Requires indemnities, escrow and representation insurance.
Seller note, earn-outBridge a price gap and keep the seller in the handover; earn-outs need clear metrics.
Partner equityOnly if the right business costs more than the $3.5M ceiling; then a written operating agreement and buy-sell first.

Succession, for a single owner

  • Incapacity: the trust owns the Holdco; a successor trustee with business authority and a durable power of attorney. There is no spouse to step in.
  • Key person: term cover owned by the business, sized to the loan; an interim manager named in writing.
  • Depth: buy a business with a capable number two, or hire a general manager within 18 months.
  • Partners: if any, a buy-sell agreement funded by insurance.
  • Estate: once value grows, non-voting units can move to a dynasty trust or GRAT, if heirs are named.
Diligence that protects the plan: a quality-of-earnings review, no customer above about 20% of revenue, key staff retained through the handover, a lease and licences that survive the sale, and a working-capital target written into the contract.

The message
“Protection is built in layers, and the order matters. Insurance first, because it is cheapest. Entities and trusts before the guarantee, because a transfer made after a claim is too late.”

What each layer stops

● InsuranceA judgment from an accident at home, on the land or in the business being paid from Kollen's own assets.
● TrustsEstate tax on the policies; probate; a court deciding who runs the business; an heir's creditor or divorce.
● EntitiesA customer, employee or tenant claim reaching beyond the entity where it arose.
● LawCreditors reaching retirement accounts, insurance values and part of the home.

Glossary · Terms and abbreviations used in this presentation

General meanings. How each applies to Kollen is covered on the pages where it appears.

APA · LOIAsset purchase agreement · letter of intentThe LOI sets the main deal terms before diligence; the APA is the binding contract to buy a business's assets.
COBRAConsolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation ActLets a departing employee keep the employer's health plan, usually for 18 months, at full cost plus 2%.
CRTCharitable Remainder TrustPays the donor an income for life or a term, then the remainder to charity; appreciated assets can be sold inside it without immediate capital gains tax.
DAFDonor-Advised FundA charitable account at a public charity: deduct when funded, recommend grants over time.
D&O · E&O · EPLIDirectors and officers · errors and omissions · employment practicesLiability insurance for decisions as an officer, professional mistakes, and employee claims.
ESOPEmployee Stock Ownership PlanA plan through which employees hold their employer's shares; here, Kollen's $3.0M German plan, and a possible exit route for his future business.
FBAR · 8938Foreign bank account report · IRS Form 8938Annual US reporting of foreign financial accounts and assets above set thresholds.
GRATGrantor Retained Annuity TrustPays the grantor an annuity for a term; growth above the IRS rate passes to heirs with little or no gift tax.
HSAHealth Savings AccountA tax-free account for medical costs, available with a high-deductible health plan.
ILITIrrevocable Life Insurance TrustOwns life insurance so the death benefit is outside the insured's taxable estate.
IULIndexed Universal Life insurancePermanent life insurance whose cash value is credited based on a market index, within caps and floors; can be borrowed against.
NQDCNonqualified Deferred CompensationAn employer plan that defers pay into later years; an unsecured promise of the employer.
NSONon-qualified Stock OptionAn option whose spread at exercise is taxed as ordinary income.
NV · SD trustNevada or South Dakota asset protection trustA trust the grantor can benefit from that those states protect from the grantor's later creditors, with an independent trustee.
QBIQualified Business Income deductionUp to 20% of pass-through business income deducted from federal taxable income.
QoEQuality of Earnings reportAn accountant's independent check of a seller's earnings before a purchase.
QSBSQualified Small Business Stock (section 1202)Lets a holder of qualifying C corporation stock exclude much or all of the federal gain on a sale.
ROBSRollovers as Business Start-upsUsing retirement funds to buy a business's stock through a new 401(k); permitted but closely scrutinized.
Rule of 55Separation from service at 55 or laterAllows penalty-free withdrawals from that employer's 401(k) after leaving in or after the year of turning 55.
SBA 7(a)Small Business Administration loan programGovernment-guaranteed bank loans up to $5M, often used to buy businesses; owners of 20% or more guarantee personally.
TODTransfer on DeathAn account designation that passes the account to named people at death without probate.
B&O · LTCBusiness and occupation tax · long-term careWashington's tax on a business's gross receipts · help with daily living over an extended period.
