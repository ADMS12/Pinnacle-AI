1 · Value and cash flow

All of the growth is investment return

Neither IRA received a contribution or paid a distribution in 2026. The only money leaving is the Merrill advisory fee, taken monthly: $4,764 year-to-date from the Rollover IRA and $130 from the Roth, which works out to about 0.68% a year on the average balance (the program's fee is deducted from cash, which is why the cash line drifts down between rebalances).

Working backwards from the year-to-date columns, the Rollover IRA started 2026 at roughly $984,891 — which matches the $0.985M December point on the statement's own chart — and the Roth at about $26,455. Since then the Rollover IRA has earned $4,933 in dividends and interest and $134,961 in market gains. The path was not smooth: the chart shows a dip to $0.971M in March before the April–May rally carried it to $1.11M, a small July pullback (−$10,769 that month), and a new high of $1.12M in August (+2.4%, in line with the S&P's 2.6% for the month).

2 · Performance

Both accounts a step ahead of the index

The Rollover IRA returned 14.2% gross (13.7% net) for January–August, and the Roth 13.7% (13.2% net). The statement prints the S&P 500 at 6,845.50 at year-end and 7,686.14 at 31 August — a 12.3% price gain, or roughly 13% with dividends — and the NASDAQ up 13.5%. A 95%-equity portfolio with a bond sliver landing at 14% gross is a fair result: in line with a broad-market mix, not a concentrated bet paying off.

Income is modest, as expected for a growth allocation: the Rollover IRA's estimated annual income of $14,049 is a 1.25% yield, and it is booked as tax-exempt inside the IRA. The account is actively rebalanced — $154,432 bought and $143,140 sold so far this year, about 14% turnover, with the excess purchases funded from the cash sweep — so the fund line-up may have changed during the year; the transaction pages would show what moved.

3 · Allocation and holdings

Growth-tilted fund portfolios, built two different ways

Both IRAs sit at 95% equities, 4% fixed income, 1% cash — an aggressive allocation for retirement money, appropriate only if the horizon is long and the owner is comfortable with 20–30% drawdowns; the March dip on the chart (about −6% in a month) is a small preview. Everything is held through mutual funds and ETFs; there are no individual stocks or bonds.

The Rollover IRA is built from actively managed mutual funds. Two Vanguard Morningstar-branded funds alone are 46% of the account, and the top five (adding a Fidelity Advisor fund, John Hancock Disciplined and JP Morgan Large Cap) are 72%. The remaining $312,577 is spread across funds the summary page doesn't name.

The Roth IRA is a textbook index portfolio: two iShares Core S&P ETFs (64%), Vanguard FTSE Developed and Emerging Markets (25% international — far more than the L-Le accounts carry), a Russell 2000 small-cap ETF, and a bond ETF. Its top five add up exactly to the equity total, so those five funds are the equity sleeve. Fees are the same rate on both accounts, but the Roth's underlying ETF costs will be much lower than the Rollover's active funds — worth asking whether the Rollover's fund line-up earns its extra cost.

4 · Observations

Worth a conversation

  • Allocation vs. stage of life. 95% equity in both IRAs is the same posture a 30-year-old accumulator would hold. If any of this money is needed within ten years, or if the owner is at or near required-distribution age, a larger bond allocation is the standard conversation to have with the Merrill advisor.
  • Concentration in two funds. Nearly half the Rollover IRA is in two Vanguard Morningstar funds. Fund-of-fund products can be well diversified internally, but the full holdings page is needed to see what they own and whether they overlap with the Fidelity, John Hancock and JP Morgan funds.
  • Cost stack. The advisory fee is about 0.68% a year; the actively managed funds in the Rollover IRA typically add 0.5–1.0% on top, versus roughly 0.05–0.1% for the Roth's index ETFs. On a $1.1M account that difference is real money every year.
  • Turnover. $154,432 of purchases against $143,140 of sales in eight months is more than routine rebalancing. Inside an IRA there is no tax cost, but it is worth knowing whether the program changed the fund line-up.
  • Roth is small relative to the Rollover. At 2.6% of the total, the Roth has little tax-diversification impact; if income and tax bracket allow, partial Roth conversions from the Rollover IRA are the usual lever — a question for the tax side of the household plan rather than the advisor.
