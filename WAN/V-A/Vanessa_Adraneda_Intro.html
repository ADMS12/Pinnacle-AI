Asset protection · Where Vanessa is exposed

Ten properties, four companies and three regulated agencies, all owned by one person with no umbrella policy on file. Most of the fixes are inexpensive; they just have to be done before a claim exists.

Personal risks

  • Lawsuits: a guest hurt at the home, a tenant or visitor at one of nine rentals, a car accident. Luke starts driving in April 2028, the largest rise in household liability in the next five years.
  • Creditor exposure: anything in Vanessa's own name answers for any judgment against her, and a personal guarantee on a lease or loan reaches all of it.
  • Single-parent risk: one earner and no second adult. A disability or a large claim lands directly on Luke's security.

Business risks

  • Several operating entities: patient-care claims, Medicare and Medicaid overpayments, employment and wage claims, caregivers driving for work.
  • Business liabilities: a Medicare recoupment can reach back years and stays with the license holder until a sale closes.
  • Cross-liability: shared staff, offices or bank accounts, cash moving between companies, or personal guarantees let a creditor argue the companies, and Vanessa, are one.

Business transition · How Vanessa moves from operator to owner to consultant

Three workstreams run side by side. Each has a few decisions that only Vanessa can make, and a few contract terms that decide whether the transition is clean.

Exit well · Pearl and Distinct

Sell for certainty, not for price

  • Cash at closing, not a 36-month management deal that keeps Vanessa the licensee and liable until 2029
  • Holdback or escrow sized to the Medicare prepayment review, so a recoupment is paid from the price, not from her savings
  • Change-of-ownership filings with Medicare and the state, on the buyer's timetable but her checklist
  • Tail cover for professional liability after the sale
  • A narrow non-compete that leaves healthcare consulting open; the most important clause for the future
  • Distinct: sell it, or fold it into Devoted if the services and licenses fit; decide by mid-2027
Result: two licenses, two payrolls and their liability off Vanessa's name by 2027
Own, don't operate · Devoted Health

Build the bench while she is still there

  • A general manager or administrator, hired or promoted in 2027, with a contract and a profit incentive
  • Monthly reporting: census, billing, collections, staffing, compliance, cash
  • An advisory board that meets quarterly; Vanessa chairs it
  • An interim manager named in the operating agreement; key-person cover on Vanessa
  • Her W-2 role ends by 2029; she is paid as owner, through distributions
  • Distribute at least the tax on its profit every year

Education planning · Luke's college, and Luke's money

College is affordable from what already exists. The planning questions are whose money pays for it, who controls what is left, and how Luke learns to handle money before he inherits any.

The cost, and what pays for it

RightCapital counts the UTMA and the savings account as a 529 and calls college 100% funded. It is funded, but by Luke's own money, which becomes his outright at 21 whatever is left. And the Simplicity transfer paperwork showed the UTMA moving into an account in Vanessa's name: a custodian cannot do that. Correct it before anything is signed.

Recommendations

1Open an Indiana CollegeChoice 529, owned by Vanessa, Luke's Trust as successor owner. $7,500 a year earns the full $1,500 Indiana credit (20%).
2Spend the UTMA on Luke's college first. It is his money for his benefit; using it reduces what passes to him unsupervised at 21.
3Name a successor custodian for the UTMA and a successor owner for Luke's IUL, so neither falls into probate.
4Leftover 529 money can roll to a Roth IRA for Luke, up to $35,000 over his lifetime, or wait for graduate school.
5No separate education trust. Luke's Trust pays for education if Vanessa is not there; the 529 does it while she is.
6At 18, Luke signs his own health-care power of attorney and HIPAA release, so Vanessa can help if he is ill at college.

Wealth transfer, in Luke's time

  • Now to 18: a custodial Roth IRA once he has a summer job; Vanessa can match his earnings
  • 18 to 25: a budget, a first credit card, a role in reviewing his own 529 and UTMA
  • 25 onward: Luke's Trust opens in stages; he joins a meeting with the trustee before each one
  • If the estate grows: yearly gifts of up to $19,000 into Luke's Trust move growth out of Vanessa's estate

Why coordination matters
“You don't need more advisors. You need one plan that lets you own one business, raise Luke, and choose your work.”
What most business owners have
A CPA for each company. An attorney for the last problem. An investment account. Insurance bought one policy at a time.
And no one whose job is the whole: no one who sees that the trust, the beneficiary forms and the insurance are one design, that a non-compete in the Pearl sale decides the consulting future, or that a teen driver changes the umbrella decision.
What the quarterback does
  • Keeps one plan, current as the businesses, Luke and the law change
  • Sequences the work: protection, exits, Devoted's bench, then the practice
  • Briefs each specialist so nothing is drafted in isolation
  • Finds the value between disciplines, where most of it lives
  • Re-tests independence every year against the age-51 goal

Glossary · Terms and abbreviations used in this presentation

General meanings. How each applies to Vanessa is covered on the pages where it appears.

529 planQualified tuition programAn education savings account: growth is tax-free when spent on education; Indiana gives a 20% credit on contributions up to $7,500 a year.
CHOW · CCNChange of ownership · CMS certification numberThe Medicare filing when a provider changes hands, and the number that identifies a certified provider.
D&O · EPLIDirectors and officers · employment practices liabilityInsurance for decisions made as a company officer, and for claims by employees.
DPOADurable power of attorneyLets a chosen agent act on financial and business matters, and keeps working if the signer is incapacitated.
GSTGeneration-skipping transfer taxA tax on transfers to grandchildren; a trust that uses the exemption can pass on to them free of estate tax.
HEMSHealth, education, maintenance, supportThe usual standard a trustee follows when paying for a beneficiary's needs.
HIPAA releaseHealth privacy authorizationLets doctors share medical information with the people named.
ILITIrrevocable life insurance trustOwns life insurance so the death benefit sits outside the insured's taxable estate.
IULIndexed universal life insurancePermanent life insurance whose cash value is credited based on a market index, within caps and floors.
Legacy trustIndiana's asset protection trustA trust Indiana allows a person to create for their own benefit that, after a waiting period, is protected from their later creditors.
LTC · DILong-term care · disability incomeHelp with daily living over an extended period · insurance that replaces earnings lost to illness or injury.
PTETPass-through entity taxA state tax paid by the company instead of the owner, deductible federally above the SALT cap.
REPSReal estate professional statusA tax status (750+ hours, more than half of working time in real estate) that lets rental losses offset other income.
RLTRevocable living trustA trust the grantor controls during life; holds assets so they pass without probate and can be managed during incapacity.
S corporationA federal tax electionProfit passes to the owner; a reasonable salary bears payroll tax and the rest is paid as distributions without it.
SALT capState and local tax deduction limitThe cap on deducting state and local taxes on a personal federal return.
SEP IRASimplified employee pensionA retirement account funded by employer contributions from a business.
Solo 401(k) · CBPOwner-only 401(k) · cash balance planRetirement plans for a business with no other employees; a cash balance plan allows much larger deductible contributions.
Trust protectorAn oversight roleA person who can replace a trustee and make limited changes to a trust's terms as circumstances change.
UmbrellaPersonal excess liability insurancePays liability claims above the limits of home, auto and landlord policies.
UPICUnified Program Integrity ContractorA Medicare contractor that reviews claims for errors and fraud and can require review before payment.
UTMAUniform Transfers to Minors ActA custodial account that belongs to the child and passes to them outright at 21 in Indiana.
