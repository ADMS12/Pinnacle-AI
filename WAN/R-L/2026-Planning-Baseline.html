What stands out

  • Margin compression. Revenue grew $113K but total deductions grew $121K, so profit fell. Ordinary income was 77% of revenue in 2023, 54% in 2024 and 24% in 2025.
  • Travel, meals and vehicles are 31% of revenue. Auto $31.7K, travel $29.6K and meals $29.2K (half deductible) total $90.5K. Plus $12.5K of vehicle depreciation and about $11K of vehicle loan interest. These lines are also the first ones an examiner reads on a sales and installation business.
  • Interest is 10.7% of revenue. $30.9K of interest, up from $734. Most of it is the Fundbox advance ($53.6K) and Headway Capital loan ($7.0K) taken in 2025 plus two vehicle loans ($93.9K). The company borrowed at high cost while lending $125K to its owner (section 2).
  • Officer salary is $13,700 on $69K of profit, $52K of distributions and $37K of new shareholder loans. That ratio is the reasonable-compensation issue the IRS targets in one-owner S corporations, and it also caps the QBI deduction once income rises (section 4).
  • $17.3K of spending was not deductible (50% of meals, all entertainment) but still reduced cash and basis.
  • No retirement plan, no health insurance, no accountable plan appear anywhere on either return.

2. Loans to shareholder

How cash moved in 2025

Balance sheet and Schedule M-2 of the 1120-S

Why it matters

The $125,100 "loan to shareholder" is the largest asset on the balance sheet (67% of total assets). It is cash the owner received that has not been taxed, because it is booked as a loan rather than salary or a distribution. The return shows no interest income on it and no repayments.

ExposureMechanicsRough cost if it happens
Recharacterized as a distributionStock basis is only $7,505, so almost all of $125K would be a capital gain (distribution in excess of basis)≈ $28K to $35K federal and CA
Recharacterized as wagesPayroll taxes at 15.3% plus income tax at the marginal rate, plus penalties≈ $19K payroll tax plus income tax
Imputed interest (§7872)Below-market loan over $10K: the company must report interest income at the federal rate (about 4%), with a matching deemed payment by the owner≈ $5K/yr of phantom income in the S corp, flows back to the owner's K-1
Interest tracingBorrowed money that funded the owner's loan is personal-use debt; the matching share of the $30.9K interest is not a business deductionUp to ≈ $12K of 2025 interest at risk

What protects the loan treatment: a signed note, a stated interest rate at or above the applicable federal rate, a repayment schedule that is actually followed, and consistent treatment on the books. None of that is visible in the return. Cleaning this up is scenario work for the next round; the choice between repaying it, converting it to salary over time, or declaring it a distribution has very different tax results.

Why the federal tax was $46

  • Itemized deductions of $49,923 took 39% of income off the top: mortgage interest $31,679, state and local taxes $14,809 (capped), charity $3,039, investment interest $396.
  • QBI deduction of $14,414 (20% of qualified business income) took another 11%.
  • Tax on the remaining $62,455 was $8,655. The residential clean energy credit (solar, carried forward from 2024) wiped out all of it.
  • The only federal tax paid was the $46 early-withdrawal penalty on a $455 IRA distribution.
  • California taxed $55,073 at $1,699 (1.3% of AGI). CA does not allow the solar credit or the QBI deduction.
  • Payroll taxes on the $49,700 of W-2 wages were $3,802 (employee share), the largest tax actually paid.

4. What $600,000 of revenue looks like with no other changes

Revenue of $600,000 is 2.07 times 2025. The result depends on what happens to costs, so three cost assumptions are shown. The middle one is the base case used below: cost of goods, outside services, permits, commissions, supplies, equipment rental and payroll processing grow with sales (they were 13.9% of revenue in 2025) and every other expense stays at its 2025 dollar amount.

Business and personal outcome by cost assumption

Personal items other than the B LLC K-1 are held at 2025 levels. What changes at $600K

    Levers this points to for the next round

    • LargeReasonable salary and the QBI wage cap. At $13,700 of W-2 wages the QBI deduction on B LLC is capped near $6,850. Raising salary costs payroll tax but raises the cap by 50 cents per dollar and funds a retirement plan. There is an optimal point.
    • LargeCalifornia pass-through entity (PTE) elective tax. The S corp pays 9.3% of the owner's share to California and deducts it federally, converting a capped SALT deduction into an uncapped business deduction. Worth roughly $9K to $12K of federal tax at the base case.
    • LargeRetirement plan through the S corp (solo 401(k) or SEP, or a defined benefit plan at this income). Each $10K contributed saves about $4,400 in combined federal and CA tax at the projected marginal rates.
    • MediumClean up the shareholder loan before the balance grows again. Decide between repayment, salary conversion, or a documented note.
    • MediumExpense discipline on travel, meals and vehicles. Every $10K cut adds $10K of taxable profit but $5.6K of cash, and lowers audit exposure. Separate the question of what is deductible from what is necessary.
    • MediumEstimated payments. 2025 federal tax was $46, so the prior-year safe harbor means almost no federal estimates are required for 2026 regardless of income; the bill is due April 2027. California's safe harbor is 110% of 2025 tax, about $1,870. This is a cash-flow planning point, not a savings.
    • SmallerHealth insurance through the S corp (deductible above the line), an accountable plan for the home office and personal vehicle, and the Section 179 / bonus decisions on any 2026 equipment.
    • SmallerRental property. $155K of suspended losses only come back against passive income or on sale. If the owner qualifies as a real estate professional, or the property is sold, that changes the picture.

    Sources and assumptions

    • Business figures: 2025 Form 1120-S pages 1 to 5, Schedule K-1, Form 1125-E, Form 4562 depreciation report, Other Deductions Worksheet, Two-Year Comparison, Five-Year Tax History, Schedule L detail, Form 100S.
    • Personal figures: 2025 Form 1040 and Schedules 1, 2, 3, A, D, E; Form 8995; Tax Summary Report; Two-Year Comparison; Federal Carryover Worksheet; Capital Loss Carryforward Worksheet; Form 540 sides 1 to 4.
    • 2026 federal brackets and the $16,100 standard deduction follow the IRS 2026 inflation adjustments as amended by the 2025 reconciliation act; the SALT cap of $40,400 and the QBI threshold of $201,750 likewise. California brackets are the 2025 tables; 2026 indexing will move them slightly.
    • The general business credit carryforward of $22,385 is assumed usable. Its origin (eligible small business credits from a pass-through) should be confirmed from the 2021 and 2022 returns.
    • Interest, dividends, capital gains, IRA, the partnership K-1 ($76,351) and the V, LLC items are held at 2025 amounts. The prior-year allowed loss of $58,496 from V, LLC was a one-time release and is also held, which is conservative; if it does not repeat, 2026 taxable income is higher by that amount.
    • Take-home is a cash view: AGI less all income and payroll taxes. It ignores non-cash items (depreciation) and the owner's personal debt service.
    • This is a baseline for scenario work, not tax advice. Numbers should be rerun by the preparer before any election is made.
