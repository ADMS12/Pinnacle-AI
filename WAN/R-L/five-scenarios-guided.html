From today's return to a protected, funded household: five scenarios, step by step

B LLC and its owner. Each scenario builds on the one before. Revenue $600,000 in 2026 and $750,000 in 2027 (25% growth). Figures come from one engine built on the 2025 federal and California returns.

How to read this page

What stays constant

  • The business. Cost of goods and the variable lines (subcontractors, permits, commissions, supplies, equipment rental, payroll processing) grow with sales at 13.9% of revenue; every other 2025 expense is held flat. That leaves a profit pool before any owner or spouse pay of $350,201 in 2026 and $479,399 in 2027.
  • Other household income of $51,695 (V C LLC wages and K-1, the V, LLC items, interest, dividends, capital loss) is held at 2025 levels. The rental loss stays suspended.
  • Credits. The energy credit ($10,543) and general business credit ($22,385) are used in 2026 and gone in 2027.
  • Rates. 2026 federal brackets (single or joint as the scenario says), California 2025 brackets, SALT cap $40,400, CA S-corp 1.5% with an $800 minimum, 4% applicable federal rate on any note. 2027 uses the same tables.

What each step adds

  1. Freeze the loan. Same structure as 2025, married filing jointly (married in 2026), spouse not working for B LLC, loan documented and repaid over time.
  2. Married, spouse on payroll. Joint filing, $12,000 W-2 for the spouse, revenue $600K, otherwise as today.
  3. Retirement, health, expansion, loan payoff. Owner $140,000 salary, solo 401(k), Roth IRAs, family health plan, $100K a year set aside in B LLC, the loan paid off in 2026 and 2027.
  4. Life insurance. Cash-value policies: $24,000 a year for the owner, $12,000 for the spouse, paid personally.
  5. Sibling C corp. A C corporation owned by the owner takes the maximum management fee, runs payroll, retirement and health, retains earnings for acquisitions, and lends the premiums and the loan payoff under split-dollar notes.

"Spendable" is the household's cash after taxes, retirement contributions, premiums, loan payments, interest and anything set aside or retained. It is the number to watch alongside "retirement funded" and "life insurance in force."

1

Freeze the loan from B LLC

What changes: nothing in the business yet. The $125,100 loan to the shareholder is frozen: no new draws, a signed note at the 4% federal rate, and $25,000 a year of principal repaid. Everything else is as filed for 2025, scaled to $600K and $750K of revenue. The owner married in 2026, so the return is filed jointly, but the spouse does not work for B LLC yet and the owner stays on a $13,700 salary.

Why freeze it

  • It is the largest asset on the balance sheet (67% of total assets) and the company has $7,505 of equity. Without the receivable B LLC is about $125K underwater.
  • None of the loan tests are met: no note, no interest, no repayment schedule, no payments, no collateral. Each year it grows the case for calling it a loan weakens.
  • Recharacterization costs real money: as a distribution, capital gain on $117,595 (about $28K to $35K plus a 20% penalty); as wages, about $19K of payroll tax plus income tax.
  • It was funded with borrowed money. The company took $60.6K from Fundbox and Headway in 2025 while lending the owner $36.9K; up to $12K of the $30.9K interest deduction is exposed under tracing rules.
  • Lenders see it. A receivable from the owner equal to two-thirds of assets is a renewal problem at the moment revenue is supposed to double.

Recommended payoff: $25,000 a year plus interest

3

Retirement plan, health insurance, expansion reserve, loan paid off in two years

What changes: owner salary $140,000, spouse $12,000. A solo 401(k): each defers $24,500 (the spouse's whole $12,000), and B LLC adds 25% of wages, $38,000 in total, for $74,500 a year into the plan. Backdoor Roth IRAs of $7,500 each. B LLC pays the $18,000 family health premium (in the owner's W-2 box 1, deducted above the line, no payroll tax). B LLC sets aside $100,000 a year of after-tax cash for expansion. The loan is repaid $62,550 in 2026 and $62,550 in 2027 through distributions within basis that the owner immediately pays back, which is tax-free because the K-1 income is already taxed.

What the policies do

  • Protection. If the owner dies, $1.5M income-tax free replaces roughly ten years of spendable income, clears the company's debt (which he has almost certainly guaranteed personally) and any remaining notes. The spouse's policy covers the loss of her contribution and final expenses.
  • Cash value. Premiums build a tax-deferred account that can be borrowed against tax free later, a second retirement reserve outside the 401(k).
  • Cost. $36,000 a year from after-tax income is about $54,000 of pre-tax earnings at the household's 33% combined marginal rate. Spendable falls to $47,614 in 2026 and $111,462 in 2027.
  • Why an S corp cannot help. Premiums a corporation pays for a more-than-2% shareholder are taxable to him, and split-dollar loans from an S corp lose their main advantage because the S corp has no separately taxed retained earnings to lend. That is what step 5 changes.
  • Bridge: a 20-year term policy of $1.5M costs under $1,000 a year and can be bound this month while the permanent policies are designed.
2026 spendable of $47,614 is not realistic to live on. In practice the set-aside or the payoff pace gives way in year 1; the point of this step is to see what full protection costs when paid personally.
Next: move payroll, benefits and the premium funding into a sibling C corporation that pays its own, lower tax and lends its retained earnings back to the family.
5

A sibling C corporation takes the management fee, retains earnings, and funds the policies and the loan payoff

What changes: the owner forms a C corporation that he owns alongside B LLC (a C corp cannot own an S corp without ending the S election). Under a services agreement the C corp provides all sales, marketing, HR and management and bills B LLC $320,000 in 2026 and $440,000 in 2027, about 90% of B LLC's profit, leaving B LLC a small K-1. The C corp employs the owner ($140,000) and spouse ($12,000), sponsors the solo 401(k), provides the health plan as a tax-free fringe benefit, and pays 21% federal and 8.84% California tax on what is left. Its retained earnings are lent to the family under documented 4% notes: the $36,000 of policy premiums first (split-dollar, with the policies assigned as collateral), then the money to repay B LLC's loan, and whatever remains is kept for acquisitions and expansion. The $100K set-aside in B LLC is replaced by the C corp's retention.

What the progression shows

  • Steps 1 and 2 are housekeeping that removes the audit exposure and brings the spouse into the business. They leave the family unprotected and unsaved.
  • Step 3 is the core. Salary, 401(k), health and the two-year payoff cut taxes below step 2 while funding $89,500 a year of retirement. Its only problem is cash in year 1, which is a pacing choice.
  • Step 4 shows the true cost of protection paid personally: $36,000 a year of after-tax money, which at the household's marginal rate is $54,000 of earnings.
  • Step 5 pays for protection and the loan payoff with corporate dollars taxed at 28% and keeps spendable near $163,202 to $148,759, at the price of a second entity, double tax on any future dividend, and $197,100 of documented debt to the family's own corporation by the end of 2027.
  • Recommended sequence: execute steps 1 to 3 now (note, payroll, 401(k), health, term life as a bridge), design the permanent policies, and form the C corp for 2027 if the owner wants to retain earnings for acquisitions and fund the policies through it.

Assumptions

  • Profit pool: revenue less variable costs at 13.9% of revenue less fixed costs of $166,592: $350,201 (2026) and $479,399 (2027). Interest savings from paying down B LLC's debt are not included.
  • Spouse: $12,000 W-2 in steps 2 to 5; her entire wage is deferred into the 401(k) in steps 3 to 5 (payroll tax still applies). Owner $140,000 in steps 3 to 5. Other payroll costs $1,000.
  • Solo 401(k): $24,500 deferral per person plus 25% employer, capped at $72,000 per person. Backdoor Roth IRAs $7,500 each, not deductible.
  • Health $18,000; life premiums $24,000 and $12,000; death benefits are placeholders pending illustrations. Loan payoff in steps 3 and 4 is funded by distributions within basis; in step 5 by C corp loans to the owner.
  • C corp: fee $320,000 and $440,000, overhead $6,000, no dividends, premiums funded before the loan payoff, remainder kept for acquisitions. Owner's interest to each lender at 4% on the opening balance.
  • Credits of $32,928 applied in 2026; none in 2027. 2027 uses 2026 tax tables. All scenarios are married filing jointly; in scenario 1 the spouse has no wages.
  • Planning analysis, not tax, legal or insurance advice. The preparer, an attorney and a licensed insurance professional should review before anything is signed.
