2 · Performance by account

The IRA led, the notes lagged — as designed

The IRA was the best performer in both periods: 13.1% in 2025 and 15.9% in the first eight months of 2026. Its two large equity funds — the Fidelity Advisor Technology fund and, since early 2026, the First Trust Rising Dividend Achievers ETF, which replaced the Franklin Rising Dividends fund at a $13,971 realized gain — did the work. Advisory fees ran 1.0% of beginning value in 2025.

The taxable advisory account returned 10.8% and 9.6%; it carries the household's largest unrealized gains ($220,857, led by Apple at +434% on cost). The joint account, with a third of its money in bond and income funds, returned 9.2% and 8.8%. The structured-notes account is a different animal: two market-linked CDs and (until July 2026) a buffered S&P note, so its return is deliberately muted (6.2% in 2025) but its downside is buffered; the BMO note matured at a $5,600 gain (20% on $28,000 over 30 months).

3 · Diversification

Growth tilt, concentrated in US large-cap technology

All four accounts carry the same RBC profile — objective Growth, risk tolerance Moderate, advisory profile 4 — and the household mix matches it: 72% equity, 16% fixed income, 6% multi-asset income funds, 5% market-linked CDs, and under 1% cash. Equity weight drifted up six points in eight months, partly from price gains and partly from 2026 repositioning: the matured BMO note's proceeds moved to the taxable account, which added new equity positions (Columbia small-cap, Global X Defense, Alger AI).

Looking through the funds, the portfolio is heavily tilted to US large-cap technology and growth: the technology sector fund (FFOMX, 11% of everything, held in both the IRA and taxable account), the Lord Abbett Growth Leaders fund, the Alger AI ETF and the individual Apple, Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, Amazon, Netflix, Texas Instruments and Qualcomm holdings together are about 39% of the total. International equity is under 2%, small-cap under 2%, and there is no real-estate or commodity exposure.

By count the portfolio looks diversified — 60 securities, an effective position count of about 22 — but the top ten holdings are 57% of the total and the same names recur across accounts (FFOMX, RDVY, LLDYX, Apple, Microsoft, Coca-Cola and JPMorgan each sit in two or three accounts). Fixed income is almost entirely short-duration corporate credit (Lord Abbett IG and high-yield funds, the Credit Suisse strategic income fund), which limits rate risk but adds credit risk, and most of the bond funds are below cost.

4 · Position performance

What moved between the two statements

Of the holdings present on both statements, Texas Instruments (+50%), the energy names (Chevron +35%, Exxon +34%), the Fidelity technology fund (+32%), Amgen, Johnson & Johnson and Coca-Cola led. The laggards were the consumer and media stocks — McDonald's, Netflix and Meta all down 13–14%, T-Mobile −11% — plus the BlackRock mid-cap growth fund. Because FFOMX is the largest holding, its 32% move alone added roughly $33,592 across the two accounts.

Bond funds show small price declines (−1.7% to −2.3%), which their 5–7% distribution yields more than offset; the chart shows price only, so total return on those is positive. Positions with no bar are new since December: RDVY (replacing FRDAX in both accounts), the Columbia small-cap fund (replacing NWKDX), Global X Defense ETF, Alphabet Class A, Honeywell Aerospace (spun off from Honeywell), Waste Management and the Citibank market-linked CD. Sold outright in 2026: Franklin Rising Dividends, Brown Advisory Growth (at a loss — likely the main source of the taxable account's −$6,576 realized), Nationwide small-cap, Accenture and Marsh & McLennan (converted to the new Marsh listing).

5 · Observations

Things worth a conversation with the advisor

  • Technology concentration. Roughly 39% of the household sits in US large-cap technology and growth, across three accounts that each hold the same tech fund. That has been the source of the returns; it is also the single risk that would hit all four accounts at once.
  • Same holdings in several accounts. FFOMX, RDVY, LLDYX, Apple, Microsoft, Coca-Cola, JPMorgan, Home Depot and McDonald's each appear in two or three accounts. The accounts are managed as one portfolio, which is reasonable, but asset location could be sharper: the highest-yielding, least tax-efficient funds (the high-yield and short-duration bond funds, the income builder fund) sit in the taxable and joint accounts, while the IRA also holds the same bond funds.
  • Fees. 2025 advisory fees were $11,147 across the three managed accounts, about 1.02% of beginning value, and 2026 is running at the same rate. The structured-notes account pays no advisory fee; its cost is embedded in the notes.
  • Bond funds under water. LLDYX, CSOIX and VFFIX are all below cost on a price basis (−5% to −18% on RBC's reinvested cost). Income has covered it, but the fixed-income sleeve is short-duration credit rather than a true diversifier against an equity drawdown.
  • Structured-notes account. The $36,000 Citibank CD bought in August 2026 is priced at $98.23 already (a −$637 mark), and the $1,000/month AutoPay continues into an account holding only illiquid notes. If the intent is to keep adding notes, fine; if not, the monthly deposit could go straight to the taxable account.
  • Cash. Under 1% of the total across all accounts; nothing idle; the structured-notes account's $34,000 cash at December was put into the Citibank CD in August.
Method

How the numbers were built

  • Source: eight RBC Wealth Management statements in WAN\L-Le\Docs\In — for each account, the December 2025 statement (this-year columns = calendar 2025) and the August 2026 statement (this-year columns = Jan–Aug 2026). Position-level data are the Asset Detail pages of each statement. Every position table reconciles to the statement's stated account value to the cent.
  • "Withdrawals" on the IRA and joint statements are quarterly advisory fees (the 2025 fee summaries match the withdrawal totals exactly for the IRA and within $2 for the other two); 2026 fees are inferred the same way (three quarterly bills to August). The joint account's 2026 withdrawals also include the $30,000 taken in May.
  • Returns: Modified Dietz with flows at mid-period, gross of fees unless labelled net. Annualized figures for the eight-month 2026 period extrapolate a partial year.
  • Position performance is price change between the two statement dates and excludes dividends and distributions; unrealized gain uses RBC's net cost, which includes reinvested distributions.
  • A companion Excel workbook (L-Le Portfolio Analysis.xlsx) holds every figure with live formulas: flows and returns, positions by account, consolidated holdings, allocation, realized trades and the fee schedule.
