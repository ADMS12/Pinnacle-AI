Shahawi Family Case: Financial Architecture

Ihab (63) and Vivian (67) hold $67.1 million, of which 99.9% sits inside the Institute of Applied Behavior Analysis (a C corporation) and two real estate LLCs. Salaries stop in 2029. The plan turns enterprise value into retirement income, moves future growth outside a taxable estate, and insures what remains, so that $25 million reaches the children and grandchildren by design rather than by a sale under pressure. Built from the RightCapital profile dated 10/16/2026 under 2026 federal and California rules.

⚠Core Blocker: $67 Million of Wealth, $100,000 of Liquidity

Fix before anything else

Everything the family owns is one company and two buildings. There is no investment account, no retirement plan, $50,000 of term life insurance and no trust. The household spends what it earns, so the Institute's $5.4 million of annual pre-tax profit stays inside a C corporation by default, where it is taxed once at 28% and again on the way out. Left alone, that cash grows to $120 million inside a $181 million estate and the IRS and probate court take $55 million first.

▭Estate Freeze Prioritization

Move the growth first, then insure what stays
$14.8M
Estate tax exposure if both died today; grows about $4M for every $10M of growth kept inside the estate
  • Step 1, housekeeping: revocable living trust, powers of attorney and health directives; retitle the Institute and LLC interests into the trust. Removes $9M of probate and manages incapacity.
  • Step 2, recapitalize and appraise: split the Institute into voting and non-voting shares; independent appraisal of the company and both LLCs. Ihab keeps the votes; the value becomes transferable at a 25% to 35% discount.
  • Step 3, freeze: SLAT for each spouse funded a year apart with discounted non-voting shares and LLC interests, using the $30M exemption; IDGT sale for the balance; GST exemption allocated so the trusts last for generations.
  • Step 4, insure the remainder: second-to-die policy of $15M to $25M inside an ILIT, premiums paid with gifts the family would make anyway; underwriting starts now at 63 and 67.
  • Step 5, give on purpose: donor-advised fund for the $825K UCLA commitment in the high-income years; direct tuition payments for the grandchildren; annual exclusion gifts of $266K a year.
Sequencing imperativeDiscounts and growth leave with the gift, so the freeze has to happen before a sale of the Institute or a jump in its value. If a buyer appears first, the pre-sale GRAT and charitable gift of shares are the only tools left, and they move far less.

⚙Planning Alternatives

The three engines of the plan; the freeze above runs under all of them
3 evaluative models
Model A · 2026 start◎

Turn the Institute into the family pension

Adopt a 401(k) with profit sharing for 2026 and a cash balance plan for 2027. Owners' contributions of $650K to $850K a year deducted at 28% in the corporation, subject to staff coverage cost from the employee census. Written distribution policy starting 2029, not 2036.

$180K to $240K of corporate tax saved a yearBuilds $2M to $3M of liquid retirement assets by 2029 · fills the 2029 to 2035 gap · staff cost typically 5% to 7.5% of eligible payroll
Model B · protection◈

Insure the people and the company

Second-to-die estate policy in the ILIT; $5M to $10M of corporate-owned key-person term on Ihab; hybrid life and long-term care policies for both, premiums paid by the Institute as an executive benefit. Premium financing considered only for the estate policy.

Liquidity at death without selling the InstituteCovers $14.8M of tax today and the residual after the freeze · replaces the $50K term policy · protects a $40M enterprise through a transition
Model C · 2027 decision▤

Decide the Institute's exit

Three paths with different tax shapes: a stock sale to a strategic or private-equity buyer (one layer of tax at about 37% federal and California); a sale of 30%+ to an ESOP with the capital gain deferred; or a family succession through discounted gifts and an IDGT sale over several years.
