How to read the architecture diagram

1 · Where assets sit today

Everything is in the middle, and almost all of it is one company

The Institute ($47M), two real estate LLCs ($20M), the home, seven properties abroad, $300K of cash and $50K of term life. No brokerage or retirement accounts; the only portfolio is inside the company. Nothing is in a trust that saves tax, and the living trust is twelve years old.

2 · Which trusts are recommended

Four now, five more only when something happens

Now: the restated living trust, SLAT 1 (Ihab, shares), the IDGT sale (Ihab, shares for a note), SLAT 2 (Vivian, LLC2 units), plus the ILIT and the grandchildren's trust. Only at a sale: the GRAT, the CRT and the CLAT, drawn with dashed borders. All irrevocable trusts are GST-exempt dynasty trusts in Nevada.

3 · Control during life

Ihab keeps every voting share; the trusts hold only value

Ihab and Vivian are trustees of the living trust and HEMS co-trustees of each other's SLAT. A Nevada trust company runs the irrevocable trusts; neither spouse is ever trustee of the trust he or she funded. Salaries, the home, the cash and the foreign property stay theirs. Each spouse can reach the other's SLAT if needed.

4 · At the first death

The living trust splits; nothing is sold, nothing is taxed

The deceased spouse's half becomes a bypass trust (the remaining exemption) and a QTIP trust (the rest, income to the survivor). The survivor keeps the voting shares and the survivor's trust. The deceased spouse's SLAT continues for the survivor. The second-to-die policy pays nothing yet; premiums continue.

5 · At the second death

The ILIT pays the tax; every trust divides four ways

The ILIT receives $20M, lends to or buys from the estate, and the executor pays about $7M of tax in cash. The purple line across the diagram is that moment: the living trust, both SLATs, the IDGT and the ILIT each divide into four equal lifetime trusts, one per child, with 20% of the estate going to the grandchildren's trust.

6 · How the children inherit

In trust for life, each on terms that fit

Nothing outright. The 21-year-old's trust has incentive terms; the London daughter's is held while she is UK resident; the 42-year-old's has broad access and he serves as the first family trustee; the divorced daughter's has an independent trustee and a spendthrift clause. HEMS, matched earnings and loans before gifts; co-trustee at 35.

7 · How the grandchildren benefit

A trust of their own, open to those not yet born

The grandchildren's trust is seeded now with LLC1 units and receives 20% at the second death. The two grandsons use it for college and, from 25, for HEMS and loans; any later grandchild joins. Each child's trust also continues to that child's descendants. No tax at the children's deaths or the grandchildren's.

8 · How the charity is fulfilled

The foundation at the center, UCLA through it

The pink dashed lines are the charitable flows: $175K a year of appreciated shares to the IABA Foundation now, the CLAT's $800K a year for twenty years at a sale, and the CRT's remainder at the second death. The foundation makes the UCLA autism research grant in the family's name, and the children and grandchildren govern it.

Protecting family wealth · Additional measures, ranked by impact

Beyond the trusts already in the plan. Each item carries an impact rating and its place in the order of work.

Business entities

  • HighHolding LLC over LLC1 and LLC2, with each major property in its own LLC. One claim, one property. Non-managing units are what SLAT 2 receives, and the extra layer raises the discount.
  • HighKeep the Institute a C corporation through the transfers; it is the liability shield for a clinical business and the only form that allows the 303 redemption and 1042 ESOP deferral.
  • MediumSeparate the $5M portfolio from operations: a documented investment account or subsidiary, so a clinical claim does not reach it.

Insurance enhancements

  • HighUmbrella and excess liability of $5M to $10M over the home, autos and 16 properties. The cheapest protection in the plan.
  • HighKey-person term on Ihab, $5M to $10M, owned by the Institute: funds a successor search and keeps lenders and buyers calm.
  • MediumBuy-sell funding once the trusts own shares: the shareholder agreement needs a funded way to buy out an estate or a trust.
  • MediumEPLI, cyber and D&O for the Institute and the foundation; review limits before a sale.

Trust enhancements

  • HighNevada situs and an independent trustee for every irrevocable trust: 365-year duration, no state income tax on what it keeps, and creditor protection that California does not offer.
  • HighSpendthrift and floating-spouse clauses in every trust; an independent trustee only for the divorced daughter's share.
  • LowerA Nevada asset protection trust for Ihab himself is possible but weak for a California resident and unnecessary once the SLATs give each spouse indirect access. Not recommended.

Family governance

  • HighTrustee succession written down: the 42-year-old with a corporate co-trustee, a trust protector, and who follows them.
  • MediumBeneficiary education: a financial literacy course by 25 and a foundation committee term by 30, as conditions of co-trusteeship.
  • MediumA family charter drafted with the children in 2027: the mission, the loan-not-gift principle, how disputes are resolved.
  • LowerPrenuptial agreements encouraged for the children; the trusts protect whether or not they sign.
