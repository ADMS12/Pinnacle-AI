1 · Executive Summary

Pinnacle Planning proposes to engage two Spanish-speaking sales/marketing partners to accelerate entry into the Spanish-speaking business-owner market — the emerging market already prioritized in the Marketing Plan (rev. 2026-08-10, Section 8). The engagement is deliberately phased: marketing first (lead generation, appointment setting, community presence), then sales (licensed production) once each partner completes licensing — targeted within approximately three months.

The structure is performance-based and self-funding: partners earn on booked, qualified appointments ($100 each) plus a 10% marketing fee, with incidentals (mileage, meals) covered — funded by diverting existing Lead Engine budget from a generic service to a culturally-fluent, in-person channel we control. After licensing, partners transition to standard FFS-level commission (40–70%), at which point they become producing salespeople, not a marketing cost.

The ask: approve the two-partner engagement, the compensation framework in Section 7, and the 90-day ramp in Section 11. No new budget is required — this reallocates existing Lead Engine spend to a higher-conviction channel.

2 · The Spanish-Speaking Market Opportunity

The Marketing Plan already identifies the Hispanic business community as our emerging market: one of the fastest-growing segments of U.S. business ownership, broadly underserved by advanced planning, with strong values alignment — family, legacy, multi-generational wealth. What the plan lacks today is native-language, in-person capacity. That is precisely the gap these two partners close.

  • Language is the trust barrier. Tax strategy, protection, and trusts are high-trust conversations. A bilingual partner who can hold that conversation in the owner's first language removes the single biggest obstacle to engagement — translation alone does not.
  • The market matches our sweet spot. Spanish-speaking owners in our territory are concentrated in exactly the entity types and profiles the plan targets: Partnership and S-Corp businesses, single-owner or husband-and-wife teams, business-savvy but without a financial support network — the "Eddie" avatar.
  • Channel synergy is already built. The Santa Ana Chamber and Hispanic Chamber presence, the Eddie-avatar flyers, and the bilingual lead magnets (Marketing Plan §8, §9) all exist or are in production. These partners are the field force that puts those assets to work.
  • First-mover advantage compounds. Referrals inside tight-knit business communities travel fast once trust is established. Two respected, Spanish-speaking representatives make Pinnacle a known name in the community before competitors arrive.
The macro trends are known. The scarcity is trusted, fluent, in-person representation — and that is what this proposal buys.

4 · Responsibilities

Phase 1 — Marketing (Primary Goal: Lead Generation)

  • Cold calling Spanish-speaking business owners using the compliance-approved, Spanish-language tax-savings script
  • Local business site visits — in-person introductions at successful local businesses; distribute Pinnacle marketing materials (drop-off package, Eddie-avatar flyers, bilingual one-pager)
  • Appointment setting — book qualified planning sessions into the advisors' calendars; confirm and remind to protect show rates
  • Represent Pinnacle at Santa Ana Chamber, Hispanic Chamber, and community events; support bilingual workshops
  • Log every contact, visit, and booking in the tracking sheet (feeds the weekly KPI meeting, Marketing Plan §12)

Phase 2 — Sales (Secondary Goal: Pipeline & Conversion)

  • Identify and qualify prospects against the Pinnacle qualification filter before booking
  • Support the pipeline: attend Meeting 1 (Discovery) alongside a lead advisor for language and trust continuity
  • After licensing: participate in the 4-meeting sales cycle as producing partners at FFS commission levels
  • Assist client onboarding and ongoing service for Spanish-preferring clients; capture referrals into the flywheel
Boundary until licensed: Phase 1 activity is strictly marketing — introductions, education, materials, and appointment setting. No product recommendations, no advice, no selling until licensing is complete. This keeps the program clean from day one.

Why This Structure Is Right

  • Pay-for-performance, not payroll. There is no base salary. Cost scales only with results (booked, qualified, shown appointments) — downside risk is limited to incidentals.
  • Better math than the Lead Engine. A $100 qualified, culturally-matched, in-person-sourced appointment compares favorably with generic purchased leads that still require setting, qualifying, and often re-qualifying. We are trading anonymous volume for warm quality — at the same budget line.
  • The 10% marketing fee aligns long-term interests. Partners benefit when their introductions become clients, so they self-select for fit rather than flooding the calendar.
  • The licensing path retains talent. The 40–70% FFS grid gives high performers a real career trajectory — the marketing phase is an audition and apprenticeship, not a dead end. Partners who produce become permanent bilingual capacity.
  • Anti-gaming guardrails: the $100 fee pays only on appointments that pass the qualification filter and show; no-shows and non-fits do not trigger payment; the definition of "qualified" is owned by Pinnacle, reviewed in the weekly KPI meeting.

  • Brand outcome: Pinnacle becomes a recognized, trusted name in the local Spanish-speaking business community — the beachhead the Marketing Plan's §8 phases were designed to build, reached faster with dedicated field capacity.
