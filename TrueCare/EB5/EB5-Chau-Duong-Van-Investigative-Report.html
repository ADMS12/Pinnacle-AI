Key takeaways
  • COVID turned a delay into a total loss. The hotel was 85 percent built and a $42 million refinance with Hall Structured Finance was days from closing on 20 March 2020 when the lockdown orders stopped it. Without that, the hotel likely opens in 2021, the senior loan is repaid, and BOH3's preferred equity is retired on schedule.
  • Prospects of recovering the investment are slim. The hotel was foreclosed for a $200,000 credit bid, Min Chae is deceased, Donald Chae is discharged, and the entity-level claims were released for $3 million shared among three entities and roughly 80 investors. Most limitation periods for direct personal claims have run or are about to.
  • The immigration objective was fully achieved. I-829 approved 15 March 2024; unconditional permanent residence for the family. This was the primary purpose of the $500,000 and it is secure regardless of anything that follows.
  • Recover the project loss through tax first, litigation last. The realistic cash recovery from the settlement is about $30,000 to $36,000 gross, paid in installments through July 2027, against net cash lost of $542,851. A capital loss on the roughly $497,000 tax basis in BOH3, harvested when the interest is worthless or the entity dissolves, is worth more at ordinary tax rates than any lawsuit against defendants who are dead, discharged, or of doubtful solvency. Pursue the low-cost member accounting demand (§8.A) alongside the tax planning; commit to litigation only if that accounting reveals a solvent target.
  • The hotel is gone and the K-1 is almost meaningless. Since the October 2021 foreclosure the hotel has been owned and operated by other, unrelated entities; BOH3 holds no interest in it. The annual K-1 showing a capital account of about $497,000 is a tax-bookkeeping carryover, not a valuation, and should not be taken as evidence that any of that value survives.

Mr. Chau subscribed on 2 September 2016 for one Class B unit of BOH3, an EB-5 "new commercial enterprise" formed to fund construction of The Source Hotel in Buena Park. BOH3 did not lend to the developer; it made a preferred equity investment in The Source Hotel, LLC ("TSH"), backed only by a "contribution agreement" under which brothers Donald and Min Chae promised to fund the return of investor capital at the end of the term. A separate assurance letter of 1 September 2016, written to his immigration consultant, restated that the Chaes were jointly and severally liable to "retire" BOH3 and promised a refund if his I-526 were denied.

The immigration side succeeded. The investment side failed. Construction stopped at roughly 85 percent in late 2019 when Evertrust Bank withheld the last $4 million of its loan; a $42 million refinance collapsed with the March 2020 COVID lockdown; Evertrust sold the note to Shady Bird Lending, which forced TSH into Chapter 11 in February 2021 and then foreclosed on the hotel leasehold on 4 October 2021 for a $200,000 credit bid. Min Chae died on 18 September 2021. Donald Chae filed personal Chapter 11 on 3 October 2021 listing about $530 million of guaranty-type debt, and his plan was confirmed on 29 June 2023 with an estimated recovery for unsecured creditors of roughly one cent on the dollar. He received a discharge on 14 July 2023.

Investor-side counsel (Edward Wu, Reid & Wise, later EDW Firm) organized the three hotel investor entities (BOH1, BOH2, BOH3), had special managers appointed, filed a $14.5 million claim for BOH3, and in 2023–2024 settled all three entities' claims against Chae, M + D Properties, MDRC and roughly 30 affiliates for $3 million total: $1 million paid up front and $2 million in 48 monthly installments ending 1 July 2027, secured by a junior deed of trust on a Pizza Hut parcel at 6832 Beach Blvd. The settlement releases the Chae parties and waives unknown claims (Civil Code §1542), but expressly does not purport to release the direct claims of individual investors who are not parties.

Mr. Chau's own attempts to recover, a refund demand to MDRC on 2 April 2024 and a follow-up on 12 July 2024, were redirected by M + D's counsel to the BOH3 special manager (Beijing Jiasheng Changqing Consulting Co., Ltd.) and its lawyer, Mr. Wu. Mr. Chau confirms that he never signed the special-manager consent or the Reid & Wise retainer, and that neither the special manager nor Mr. Wu has ever written to him, offered him the distribution ballot that BOH2 investors received in September 2024, or accounted for BOH3's share of settlement receipts. Meanwhile BOH3's K-1s, still prepared from MDRC's post office box, carry his capital account at about $497,000 as if the investment were intact.

Bottom line. The hotel is gone, the guarantor brothers are respectively deceased and discharged, and the entity-level claims are released. The realistic recovery paths are (1) forcing an accounting and pro-rata distribution of BOH3's settlement share, (2) monitoring the M + D note for default, which would revive up to $15 million of entity claims, and (3) evaluating any direct, unreleased personal claims against solvent parties before limitation periods close, several of which may already have run. Section 8 ranks these.

4. What transpired, in four acts

Act 1 · Subscription and funding (2016–2019)

Mr. Chau came to the project through RMI, a Vietnamese-language immigration consultancy with offices in San Jose, Westminster and Ho Chi Minh City. RMI's contract disclaims any promise about outcome and charges $25,000 or 5 percent of the investment. The suitability questionnaire records that the $500,000 represented about 20 percent of the family's net worth and that he had "some" experience only with real estate; every other asset class is marked "none." He nonetheless certified accredited-investor status and that he could bear a total loss.

Two features of the paperwork matter later. First, the 1 September 2016 assurance letter was written by MDRC to RMI, naming Mr. Chau, one day before he signed. It promises that the Contributors will be "jointly and severally liable to contribute enough capital … to retire the Company" and that a final I-526 denial triggers a 60-day refund. A promise of return of capital sits uneasily with the EB-5 "at risk" rule, which Chae's own disclosure statement later cites for the proposition that "the individual investors are not creditors of the Debtor." Second, the wire confirmation shows $555,000, not $500,000. Mr. Chau confirms the extra $55,000 was paid as service fees to RMI and MDRC. The split between them and the invoices are not in the file; the assurance letter's refund promise covers "the administration fee (if any)," so the MDRC portion was part of the bargain the Chaes stood behind.

For three years the investment behaved. BOH3 allocated income of roughly $10,000 to $15,000 a year and paid two small cash distributions. The family obtained conditional permanent residence and moved to Pennsylvania.

Act 2 · Distress (late 2019 – early 2021)

The hotel was 85 percent built when Evertrust Bank refused the last $4 million draw. A replacement lender was at the closing table in March 2020 when the lockdowns hit. Evertrust then sued the Chaes on their guaranties and, in December 2020, sold the note to Shady Bird Lending, a distressed-debt buyer, which immediately moved to foreclose and to install a receiver. TSH's Chapter 11 filing on 26 February 2021 bought seven months.

Act 3 · Collapse (2021)

The bankruptcy court approved a marketing process ending in an auction on 30 September 2021. Two bidders offered about $22 million, but neither posted a qualifying deposit, and the sale motion was denied. On the investor side, Mr. Wu's coalition wanted to credit-bid the EB-5 debt with a partner, which required replacing MDRC as manager of BOH1 and BOH2; the record shows he could only obtain the investor list by court order because the Chae-controlled manager would not supply it. Nothing in the file shows a credit bid was made. Min Chae died on 18 September. Donald Chae filed Chapter 11 on Sunday 3 October, inside a tolling window negotiated with Shady Bird, to strip Shady Bird's attachment liens. Shady Bird foreclosed the next morning for a $200,000 credit bid against a $31.9 million claim. Two weeks earlier, MDRC's letter had still been asking investors to put in more money.

Act 4 · Bankruptcy, settlement and silence (2021–2026)

The three BOH entities sat on the Creditors' Committee and filed claims totalling $36 million. They obtained one extension to bring a fraud-based nondischargeability complaint but, on this record, never filed one; instead they signed a term sheet on 1 June 2023 and voted for the plan. The plan itself paid general unsecured creditors about a penny on the dollar from a trust funded largely by non-estate Malibu properties. The side settlement gave the BOH entities $3 million over four years and released Chae, M + D, MDRC and about thirty affiliates, with a §1542 waiver of unknown claims. If M + D misses a payment and fails to cure within 30 days, the entities may reassert up to $5 million each, less amounts paid, with limitation periods tolled.

Mr. Chau's immigration case closed successfully in March 2024. His two refund letters were answered only by M + D's lawyer, who pointed him to the special manager. The BOH2 investors received an accounting and a ballot in September 2024; Mr. Chau confirms he received nothing comparable, that he never signed the special-manager consent or the Reid & Wise retainer, and that his 12 July 2024 letter drew no reply from the special manager or Mr. Wu.

6. The Chae bankruptcy and the settlement

How the BOH entities fared in the plan

The Chae plan placed the BOH entities in Class 7, general unsecured claims, estimated at $267.6 million in total, to share pro rata in a Plan Trust the debtor valued at about $2.68 million. BOH3's $14.5 million claim would, on those numbers, be worth on the order of $145,000 before the plan's offset rule: the "Allowed GUC Distribution Amount" is reduced dollar-for-dollar by anything the creditor receives from the primary obligor, other guarantors, or third-party collateral. Settlement payments from M + D likely count as such "Other Distributions," so BOH3's trust recovery may be small or nil. The file does not show whether the claim was allowed or whether any trust distribution has been made.

The settlement in one picture

Terms that matter for Mr. Chau

  • Payments and default (§§1–2, Note §2). Installments are due the first business day of each month. Default requires written notice by email to Womble; a 30-day cure period follows. Only after an "Uncured Default" may the entities enforce or reassert claims. Someone must actually be watching the wire log and sending notices; investors have no direct standing.
  • Security (§§3–4). A junior deed of trust on the Pizza Hut parcel at 6832 Beach Blvd., Buena Park (APN 276-323-01), behind a $1,006,000 first lien to J & K PLC recorded 12 Apr 2022. The entities "may record" it at any time. Whether it was in fact recorded is not shown.
  • Releases (§§8–10). Sweeping, but given by the entities only. Section 15 states that nothing "shall be construed as a representation by a BOH Investor Entity that it has the authority to release the direct claims of any party who is not a Party." Section 17 lets M + D terminate as to any entity whose special manager lacked authority, with disgorgement of payments. An investor who challenges the special manager's authority therefore risks unwinding the entity's recovery.
  • Immigration (§7). The Chae parties promise only to "continue to provide assistance to the extent that they can." Mr. Chau no longer needs this.
  • Allocation (§19). The split among the three entities and among their members is entirely the entities' responsibility. This is where his leverage lies: he is a member entitled to an accounting.
  • Confidentiality (§29). The agreement may be shown to members "with the instruction … to keep this Agreement and its terms confidential." He is entitled to a copy.
  • Fee-shifting (§22). Prevailing party recovers attorneys' fees in any action to enforce or interpret the agreement.

Direct claims unassessed, clock running

Mr. Chau did not file a proof of claim in the Chae case, is not a party to the settlement, and has released no one. Whether any personal claim survives against a solvent party turns on limitation periods that began running, at the latest, in 2021. See §8.

8. Alternative remedies

Ranked by expected value net of cost. Remedies A and B cost little and should be pursued regardless; C through F require a decision about litigation budget and are subject to limitation-period analysis that should be done first.

A. Enforce member rights inside BOH3 low cost · highest probability

Mr. Chau remains a Class B member. California's Revised Uniform LLC Act (Corp. Code §17704.10) gives a member the right, on written demand, to inspect and copy the operating agreement, the member list, tax returns, financial statements and "books and records," and to be informed of matters affecting the company. The 23 July 2024 Womble letter is itself an admission that the special manager and Mr. Wu are the people who must answer. Because Mr. Chau never signed the special-manager consent or any retainer, he is not Mr. Wu's client and owes the special manager no deference; conversely, the special manager owes him, as a non-consenting member, the same fiduciary duties of candor and equal treatment it owes the members who appointed it.

  1. Serve a written demand on the Special Manager, care of EDW Firm (4630 Center Blvd. #903, Long Island City, NY 11109; edward.wu@edwlawgroup.com), for: the executed Settlement Agreement, Term Sheet, Note and recorded Junior Deed of Trust; the written consents by which the special manager was appointed under §5.2.4; BOH3's proof of claim no. 6 and any Plan Trust correspondence; a ledger of every settlement receipt and its allocation among BOH1/2/3; the engagement letter and fee arrangement with EDW Firm; the current member list; and the explanation for the 2022 §481(a) adjustment and the 2024 membership change.
  2. Demand the same distribution election the BOH2 investors were offered on 10 September 2024, or an explanation of why BOH3 investors were not.
  3. Ask whether default notices have ever been sent to M + D and whether every installment has been received on time. A missed installment is the single event that revives up to $15 million of claims, and the entities must send the notice to trigger it.
  4. If the demand is refused, Corp. Code §17704.10(g) authorizes a court order compelling inspection with attorneys' fees to the member. Alternatively the Operating Agreement's §10.9 arbitration clause may govern.

Expected outcome: a pro-rata share of BOH3's settlement receipts, roughly $30,000 to $36,000 gross over the payment term, plus transparency that informs every other option.

B. Organize the BOH3 members low cost

A majority of Class B members appointed the special manager and can replace it or direct it. Once the member list is obtained, the Vietnamese-speaking investors introduced through RMI (the assurance letter refers to "investors … introduced by the Client," suggesting Mr. Chau brought others) are natural allies. Collective action also spreads the cost of any of the remedies below across 28 investors.
