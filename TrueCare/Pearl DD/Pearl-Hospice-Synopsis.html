Pearl Hospice LLC: company synopsis

Licensed, Medicare-certified hospice serving Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake and Will counties, Illinois. Offered for sale with immediate transfer of operations.

Pearl Hospice is a freestanding, Medicare-certified hospice with a clean Illinois license through July 2027, a provider agreement dating from 2019, and a five-county Chicago-area service territory. The owner has decided to exit healthcare operations and is seeking a buyer who will take over day-to-day operations and regulatory responsibility now, not at the end of a multi-year management term. In return the seller is prepared to price the business for a quick, clean closing.

Matters a buyer should plan for

The seller is disclosing these up front so that offers reflect them and diligence holds no surprises.

  1. Medicare prepayment review. On June 10, 2026 the Midwest Unified Program Integrity Contractor (CoventBridge) placed the agency on prepayment medical review of all claims. Six additional documentation requests issued September 24, 2026 covering May through July 2026 service months ($36,094 in charges) are due November 8, 2026. No reason or error rate was stated. The seller will fund and deliver the ADR responses and will carry the resulting liability for pre-closing dates of service.
  2. Census. Average daily census fell from about 11 in 2025 to the low single digits in 2026. The licensed capacity, contracts and referral history remain; volume must be rebuilt.
  3. Staffing. Current payroll is seven people. A buyer should plan to place its own administrator, director of nursing and volunteer and bereavement coordinators.
  4. Documentation to be completed. The seller is assembling the CMS tie-in for the 2022 change of ownership, the operating agreement reconciling the ownership percentages across CMS, IDPH and tax filings, the medical director agreement, and current insurance certificates.
  5. Affiliated business. The owner also operates a home health and staffing company (Devoted Healthcare) at the same address. Shared services will be separated at closing and the seller will sign a non-compete covering hospice in the five counties.

Proposed transaction: operations and liability transfer at signing

The seller's objective is to be out of hospice operations and regulatory responsibility as soon as the law allows. A conventional management-agreement-with-option, in which the seller holds the license for three years while a manager runs the business, does not meet that objective and will not be accepted. The structure the seller is offering moves control, cost and risk to the buyer in stages, each as early as the rules permit.

Why a buyer should prefer this structure. The buyer controls operations from the first day and can start rebuilding census on its own referral network without waiting for regulatory approval. It acquires a licensed, enrolled Medicare hospice in a no-CON state for the cost of a shell, with the prepayment-review history ring-fenced behind a holdback, and it avoids the fraud-and-abuse exposure that a profit-share management arrangement carries for both sides.

Process

  1. Sign the non-disclosure agreement and receive data-room access: 37 documents covering corporate records, licensure and enrollment, 2022 to 2025 tax returns and financial statements, payroll, vendor agreements and the 2026 prepayment-review correspondence.
  2. Management call with the owner and administrator; site visit on request.
  3. Indicative offer letter stating price, structure (asset or membership interest), option fee, holdback and timing.
  4. Selected buyer: interim management agreement and definitive purchase agreement negotiated concurrently; target signing within 30 days of selection.

The seller intends to select a buyer in the fourth quarter of 2026 so that the interim management period begins before year end and the change of ownership is approved in the first quarter of 2027.
